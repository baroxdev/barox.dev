I love sharing what I know. But for a long time I told myself I wasn't ready — that with only a few years of experience, nobody would want to hear from me. I decided I'd start writing someday, once I was a senior, a principal engineer, maybe even a founder. This is the post where I explain why I stopped waiting.

What changed wasn't a promotion or some milestone. It was realizing that "a few years of experience" is still experience — progress, mistakes, lessons learned, plain perspective, all of it real, even if it isn't senior-level wisdom. Someone out there is exactly where I was a year ago: a beginner, or just lost, looking for one more perspective. That's who I'm writing for, and I don't need a title to be useful to them.

Knowing who I was writing for didn't automatically make me write, though. The next trap was different: I told myself I'd start once I had a portfolio's worth of drafts ready — enough ideas stacked up that I'd never stare at a blank page. All that did was turn "write something" into "plan everything," and planning doesn't publish itself. Too much thinking, not enough doing.
